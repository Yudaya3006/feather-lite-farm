What Are Quail Eggs?

Quail eggs are small, beautifully speckled eggs laid by quail birds. Renowned for their delicate flavor, rich creamy yolk ratio, and bite-sized aesthetic, they are a beloved ingredient across home kitchens, gourmet dining, and global cuisines. Whether served soft-boiled on salads, fried as delicate canapés, or pickled for appetizers, quail eggs bring culinary elegance to any table.

Culinary Appeal

Why Choose Quail Eggs

Discover the unique features that make quail eggs a favorite among chefs and food lovers.

🟡

Rich Yolk Ratio

Quail eggs feature a proportionally larger yolk compared to their egg white, providing a velvety, rich texture in every bite.

⏱️

Fast & Easy Boiling

Because of their compact size, quail eggs cook quickly — soft boiling in just 2 minutes and reaching a full hard boil in 3 to 3.5 minutes.

✨

Bite-Sized Presentation

Their charming speckled shells and delicate size make them perfect for impressive party platters, finger foods, and salad toppings.

Kitchen Inspiration

Uses of Quail Eggs

Simple and creative ways to incorporate fresh quail eggs into everyday meals.

🥗

Salads & Appetizers

Halved soft-boiled quail eggs add rich texture and visual charm to Cobb salads, Caesar salads, or gourmet tapas.

🍜

Soy-marinated or soft-boiled quail eggs serve as an authentic, savory topping for hot ramen broth and noodle dishes.

🥪

Canapés & Mini Sliders

Sunny-side-up fried quail eggs fit perfectly on crostini, mini sliders, avocado toast, and party appetizers.

Pickled & Preserved

Hard-boiled quail eggs are ideal for pickling in spiced vinegar solutions, creating delicious long-lasting snacks.

Refrigeration & Freshness Tips

Keep eggs stored inside their carton between 35°F–40°F (2°C–4°C) pointed-end down. This keeps the air pocket at the top and helps preserve freshness.

* Boiling tip: Submerge in cold water first or gently lower with a spoon into simmering water for 2–3 minutes.
